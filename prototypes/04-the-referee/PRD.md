# THE REFEREE
## Product Requirements — Democratising Data Centres

**Working line (canon):** democracy is the referee for social impact  
**This build:** a night session of a civic officiating booth. Not a score. Not a stadium game.  
**Tracks:** Voloridge / Arrowstreet — a technical aha that treats public language as a time series.  
**Do not confuse with:** the factory-twin cluster. That is a different prototype.

---

## 1. Vision

A data-centre campus is a fixture that arrives in a place that already had a water book, a grid queue, a school levy, and a night-time noise floor. The public is told to "have a say." The operator is told to "engage." Both are handed a comment portal and a 90-day clock. That is not officiating. That is a suggestion box next to a pouring.

THE REFEREE puts a person in the middle chair of a long table and makes them do the job democracy actually has: hear two benches, write reasons, attach conditions, or abandon the fixture. The product is the process made visible. The AI is a clerk with a lamp, not a judge with a score.

If this works, a resident in Quincy and a desk officer at an OECD working party sit in the same booth. One of them is closer to the well. The other is closer to the 20-year water account. They do not get different facts. They get different chairs.

---

## 2. The referee thesis

Democracy is not an impact score. A score is a private referee: a model that pretends the fight is over because a number landed. THE REFEREE refuses that. The public process *is* the referee — slow, written, appealable, and obliged to say why.

An LLM can ingest the news and flag the campuses. It cannot substitute for the night someone has to sit between Operator and Parish and write the reasons in ink. Sensemaking (the Jigsaw move) is useful only when it is *disclosed on request*: what the benches already agree on, which the model was going to keep as a professional secret. Redesign is not a vibe. It is a substituted play — a condition that must enter the laws of the game or the fixture is abandoned.

Three sentences we will say to a judge without blinking:

1. Data-centre siting is a match that already has two benches; the missing official is the public process.
2. We do not score the campus. We force a call — play on, advantage, yellow with a bound condition, or red — and we write the reasons live.
3. The technical aha is a regime-change detector on the public record: the date local language left "jobs" and arrived at "aquifer."

---

## 3. Users

**The parish (general public).** People who live with the well, the easement, the night fans, the PILOT that never reached the school. They do not want a dashboard. They want their claim heard as testimony, not dissolved into a sentiment donut. They should be able to officiate a real-feeling fixture in their region in one sitting and leave with a shareable condition.

**The operator's public-affairs bench.** Not the villain costume. The bench that has to put three claims on the table and live with a yellow. If they cannot bind a water cap or a shared tap, they should see the red coming.

**The policymaker.** A person who must interface with the same fight at a different horizon: 20-year water, 20-year grid, intergenerational incidence. They do not get a different product, a "Pro" tier, or a classified briefing. They get the end chair and an OECD-style one-pager built from the *same* ingested facts. If a fact is not in the match report, it is not on the one-pager.

**The clerk (us, tonight).** The team running the booth. We do not hide the sensemaking. We hold it until someone asks, because that is the product claim.

Out of scope as primary users: traders, site-selection consultants, the factory-twin operators. They have another table.

## 4. Journeys

### 4.1 Night session — parish or any public official of the process

1. Open the fixtures book. Four regions, geolocated: Mid-Columbia, Northern Virginia, Salt River Valley, Licking County. Each fixture is a campus fight with a named vice (water, transmission, heat/CAP, PILOT).
2. Take a fixture. The match report is already written from timestamped dispatches — the "LLM ingested the news" step, shown as a clerk's pile, not a chat.
3. Hear three claims from Operator, three from Parish. Spoken-word cards. No paragraphs of UI copy pretending to be testimony.
4. Officiate. Play on / advantage / yellow / red. Each call writes a line into the REASONS. The reasons are legal-poetic on purpose: a public document, not a toast.
5. If yellow: the substitution board will not clear until a redesign condition is attached (setback, water cap, shared substation, community benefit). That condition becomes a Laws of the Game card, persisted in localStorage, copyable.
6. Walk the forum. Spectators throw bottles of commentary (preloaded, plus a shout). After three shouts the hidden agreement is disclosed. That is the Jigsaw move we refuse to keep backstage.
7. Read the surprise index. See the date the nouns changed.

### 4.2 End chair — policymaker

Toggle the brass plaque. The body slides to the end of the table. Benches dim. The same fixture becomes a one-pager: matter before the desk, 20-year water book, 20-year grid book, intergenerational incidence, what the public process has already bound. No new megawatts, no new acre-feet, no "and also we found." The chair changed. The facts did not.

### 4.3 After the lamp

A yellow card with a condition can be carried out of the room as a card. A red can be carried as written reasons. Nothing is a leaderboard.

## 6. Why policymakers get a different chair, not a different product

A second product is how you lose the public. The moment the OECD page has a fact the parish cannot see, the booth is a briefing room and the forum is theatre.

Policymakers need a different *grammar*: acre-feet in 2046, interconnection queues, who eats the stranded substation, what a 30-year PILOT does to a levy. That grammar is a chair at the end of the same table. The one-pager is allowed to reorder and to lengthen. It is not allowed to invent. If the parish heard "1.4 billion gallons," the desk officer reads the same number as a 20-year book, not a bigger number.

This is also how we stay honest about OECD.aim and the longterm wiki: those rooms already know how to write long. They do not need a new dataset. They need the public process bound in a form they can put in a folder.

---

## 7. The undisclosed-sensemaking aha

Jigsaw-style sensemaking is usually a backstage professional object: a cluster map the facilitators see and the room receives as a summary. THE REFEREE treats that secrecy as the thing to break.

The model is allowed to notice agreement early. It is not allowed to perform it until the forum has made three noises. Then the lamp shows the carbon line: *both benches already agree on X; the fight is Y.*

That delay is the product. If we disclosed on load, we would be the private referee again — the score that arrives before the hearing. If we never disclosed, we would be the comment portal. The aha for a technical judge (Voloridge / Arrowstreet) is adjacent: once you can timestamp the public record, you can detect a *regime change in nouns*. "Jobs" giving way to "aquifer" is not sentiment. It is a structural break. Traders already look for those in prices. Civic process almost never looks for them in language. We do, in a strip the width of a ticker, because that is the whole trick.

## 8. Non-goals

- A numeric social-impact score, ESG badge, or "community happiness" donut.
- A sports video game, a scoreboard of fans, or a home-team parish. The match metaphor is officiating, not entertainment.
- A chatbot that "explains data centres."
- Live news ingest, live geolocation, or a real LLM in this prototype. The pipeline is staged as already-ingested. The interface must make that staging obvious (timestamps, dispatch stack), not fake a live crawl.
- The factory-twin / industrial digital-twin cluster.
- Site-selection tooling for operators.
- A map product. Geoloc is a fixtures list, not a GIS flex.
- Accounts, auth, npm, a build step.
- Resolving a real siting fight tonight. We are building the booth that would make that fight officiable.

## 9. Ninety-second judge path

0:00 — Lamp. Title card. Read the line: you are the process, not the crowd. Enter.  
0:10 — Fixtures book. Four regions. Open Mid-Columbia → Cascade Ridge Phase III.  
0:20 — Point at the match report (dispatches). Point at the surprise index: June 2023, aquifer overtakes jobs. That is the Voloridge sentence.  
0:35 — Hear one Operator claim, one Parish claim (keyboard or click).  
0:45 — Yellow. Substitution board blocks you until you attach a water cap. Reasons type themselves.  
1:00 — Throw two bottles, shout a third. Hidden agreement discloses: both benches want the well metered.  
1:15 — Brass plaque. Chair slides. OECD one-pager, 20-year water, same gallons.  
1:25 — Copy the Laws of the Game card. Close.

If a judge only has forty seconds: yellow + condition + surprise-index cross + plaque. That is the product.

## 10. Risks — do not trivialise communities as a game

The most available failure is aesthetic success. Oxblood and a match clock can make Quincy look like a derby and the people on the canal look like a firm. That is a political injury, not a UX miss.

**In the product.** We use "Parish" as a bench of record (irrigation district, school board, HOA, town), not a mascot. Claims are testimony. Bottles are commentary thrown at the *process*, labelled as such. Yellow and red are administrative acts with written reasons. The title card forbids officiating for sport. There is no score, no winner animation, no crowd meter.

**In the room.** A judge who laughs at the red card has to be walked back to the sentence it writes: the expansion is refused; the campus as built may stand; the next hall may not. If that sentence feels like a joke, the booth failed.

**In the world.** A prototype cannot irrigate a canal. It can refuse to turn the people who still can into a home crowd. The risk is not that we are "too playful." The risk is that play becomes a way to not listen. The reasons sheet exists to make listening leave a mark.

**Secondary risks.** Staged news treated as live. Policymaker chair that quietly grows new facts. Sensemaking that sounds like both-sides. A yellow condition that is theatre (visitor centre) rather than a bind (meter, cap, tap, setback). We design against all four: timestamps as archive, one-pager with no novelty, agreement named as agreement, conditions named as binds.

## 11. Requirements for this prototype (hackathon night)

Must:

- Open as a single HTML file. Inline CSS and JS. Google Fonts CDN allowed. No npm.
- Four regions, one fixture each, real-feeling (water, transmission, CAP/heat, PILOT).
- Match clock; substitution board; physically thick yellow/red.
- Spoken-word claims, three per bench.
- Four calls: play on, advantage, yellow, red. Yellow requires a condition. Reasons update live.
- Forum: six or more preloaded bottles plus a shout. Sensemaking after three.
- Laws of the Game card in localStorage, copyable.
- Policymaker plaque: same fixture, OECD one-pager, chair slides.
- Surprise index with a visible regime-change date.
- Keyboard: fixtures, calls, shout box.
- Copy that states the non-game rule in the booth, not only in this document.

Must not:

- Purple glass SaaS. Kanban. Inter/Roboto/Arial/Space Grotesk/system-ui.
- A 12-column card grid.
- A factory twin.
- A score.

---

## 12. Success tonight

A stranger can officiate Cascade Ridge, bind a water cap, see the 2023 noun-break, disclose the hidden agreement, and slide to the OECD chair — and explain, in one breath, why none of that was a score.
